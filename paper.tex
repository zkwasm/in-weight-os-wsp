\begin{abstract}
Adapting pretrained Large Language Models (LLMs) to multiple downstream capabilities---such as surgical safety alignment, structured multi-tool invocation, and adaptive System-1/System-2 compute routing---currently relies on Supervised Fine-Tuning (SFT), Reinforcement Learning (RLHF/DPO/GRPO), or post-hoc model merging (\texttt{Task Arithmetic}, \texttt{TIES-Merging}). However, gradient-based updates modify dense, overlapping singular subspaces across layers, giving rise to two fundamental pathologies: (1) \textbf{Intra-Task Over-Triggering} (e.g., tool-calling SFT models hallucinate tool invocations on $28\%\text{--}100\%$ of standard mathematical reasoning queries, while safety SFT models falsely refuse $20\%\text{--}80\%$ of benign technical prompts containing sensitive keywords on \texttt{XSTest}); and (2) \textbf{Inter-Task Destructive Interference}, where sequential continual SFT suffers catastrophic forgetting and multi-task weight merging degrades individual skills by $25\%\text{--}66\%$.

In this paper, we introduce \textbf{In-Weight OS}, a gradient-free post-training paradigm that compiles multiple independent behavioral programs into \textbf{strictly disjoint dormant SwiGLU channels} ($\mathcal{C}_1 \cap \mathcal{C}_2 \cap \dots \cap \mathcal{C}_K = \emptyset$) of the terminal Transformer MLP layer ($\mathrm{MLP}_{L-1}$). Grounded in terminal-layer KV-cache invariance and \texttt{bfloat16} residual mantissa absorption ($\mathrm{fl}_{\mathrm{bf16}}(h + \Delta h) \equiv h$ for gate pre-activations $z \le -15.0$), we formulate a \textbf{2-Fold Out-of-Fold (OOF) Shrinkage-Calibrated Guard-Nullspace Compiler} that places each skill's activation boundary orthogonal to both general reasoning manifolds and all sibling skills. Across \textbf{\texttt{Qwen3-8B}} ($L=36$) and \textbf{\texttt{Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}} ($L=32$), In-Weight OS compiles three distinct post-training skills into just $51\text{--}57$ dormant neurons ($\sim 0.014\%$ of model parameters) in $12\text{--}15$ seconds with \textbf{zero gradient steps}, achieving $98.33\%\text{--}100.0\%$ multi-skill primary success with near-zero collateral false triggers, outperforming Sequential Continual SFT by $+65.0\%$, \texttt{Task Arithmetic} by $+46.7\%$, and \texttt{TIES-Merging} by $+24.6\%$.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Post-training transforms a generalist pretrained LLM into a safe, tool-capable, and compute-efficient assistant. In standard practice, each post-training skill is acquired via gradient descent---either sequentially (Continual SFT / RL) or via separate adapters that are subsequently combined in weight space (\texttt{Task Arithmetic} \cite{ilharco2023}, \texttt{TIES-Merging} \cite{yadav2023}).

Yet gradient-based post-training suffers from a fundamental geometric flaw: stochastic gradient descent lacks an algebraic mechanism to confine parameter updates to a trigger-specific activation manifold. When fine-tuned on $16\text{--}30$ examples of a specialized behavior (e.g., emitting JSON tool calls or refusing harmful requests), rank-unconstrained or LoRA updates shift the global bias of the residual stream:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Exaggerated Safety (\texttt{XSTest} Over-Refusal)}: Safety-tuned models latch onto surface lexical triggers (\texttt{kill}, \texttt{terminate}, \texttt{dump}, \texttt{exploit}, \texttt{crack}), refusing $20\%\text{--}80\%$ of benign systems-programming and mathematical questions.
    \item \textbf{Hallucinated Tool Invocations}: Models fine-tuned on function-calling schemas over-trigger \texttt{<tool\_call>} on $28\%\text{--}100\%$ of pure mathematical derivations (\texttt{AMC}, \texttt{MATH-500}).
    \item \textbf{Multi-Adapter Subspace Collision}: When combining a Safety adapter ($\Delta W_{\mathrm{safe}}$), a Tool-Calling adapter ($\Delta W_{\mathrm{tool}}$), and a Fast-Routing adapter ($\Delta W_{\mathrm{fast}}$), parameter-space heuristics (\texttt{Task Arithmetic}, \texttt{TIES-Merging}) cannot prevent activation-space cross-talk: safety weights falsely block SQL/Python tool queries containing words like \texttt{dump} or \texttt{execute}, while tool and fast-routing weights overwrite safety refusal tokens.
\end{enumerate}

We resolve both pathologies by treating the terminal gated MLP ($\mathrm{MLP}_{L-1}$) as a **multi-tenant, channel-partitioned operating system (\emph{In-Weight OS})**.

\section{Theory: Orthogonal Dormant-Channel Superposition}

Let $\mathrm{MLP}_{L-1}(x) = \sum_{c=1}^{d_{\mathrm{ffn}}} w_{\mathrm{down}, c} \, \sigma(\langle w_{\mathrm{gate}, c}, x \rangle) \langle w_{\mathrm{up}, c}, x \rangle$ denote the terminal gated MLP layer. Suppose we wish to install $K$ distinct post-training skills $\mathcal{S}_1, \dots, \mathcal{S}_K$, where each skill $m$ is defined by an active state manifold $\mathcal{X}_+^{(m)} \subset \mathbb{R}^{d_{\mathrm{model}}}$ (partitioned into $N_m$ discrete state-transition sub-manifolds $\mathcal{X}_{+,s}^{(m)}$ emitting target tokens $t_{m,s}$) and a protected out-of-skill manifold $\mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{guard}}^{(m)}$.

\begin{theorem}[Zero-Interference Multi-Skill Superposition]
\label{thm:superposition}
Suppose $K$ skills are compiled into pairwise disjoint dormant channel subsets $\mathcal{C}_1, \dots, \mathcal{C}_K \subset \mathcal{D}_{L-1}$ ($\mathcal{C}_m \cap \mathcal{C}_{m'} = \emptyset$ for all $m \ne m'$). If for every skill $m$ and channel $c \in \mathcal{C}_m$, the gate vector $w_{\mathrm{gate},c}$ satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\langle w_{\mathrm{gate},c}, x \rangle \le -15.0 \quad \forall x \in \mathcal{X}_{\mathrm{general}} \cup \bigcup_{m' \ne m} \mathcal{X}_+^{(m')},
\end{equation}
then under IEEE 754 \texttt{bfloat16} residual addition, for any input $x \in \mathcal{X}_+^{(m^*)}$ belonging to skill $m^*$, all channels $c \in \bigcup_{m' \ne m^*} \mathcal{C}_{m'}$ are absorbed bit-for-bit into the residual stream ($\mathrm{fl}_{\mathrm{bf16}}(h^{(L-1)} + \Delta h_c) \equiv h^{(L-1)}$), and the superposed model executes skill $m^*$'s state machine identically to a single-skill model.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Out-of-Fold (OOF) Shrinkage-Calibrated Guard-Nullspace Solver}
When compiling an entry gate ($s=0$) from a small calibration set $X_+ \in \mathbb{R}^{N_+ \times d_{\mathrm{model}}}$ ($N_+ \ll d_{\mathrm{model}}$), unregularized sample covariance overfits to the $N_+$-dimensional calibration subspace, causing unseen test projections $\langle k, x_{\mathrm{test}} \rangle$ to shrink by a factor $\rho \in [0.45, 0.75]$ relative to in-sample projections.

To guarantee that unseen test prompts fire reliably while maintaining zero false triggers on sibling skills and lexical lookalikes ($X_{\mathrm{guard}}$), we:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Balance Semantic vs.\ Mid-FSM Negatives}: We construct the class-balanced data matrix $X_p = [0.85 X_{\mathrm{neg,sem}}/\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{sem}}};\; 0.50 X_{\mathrm{neg,fsm}}/\sqrt{N_{\mathrm{fsm}}};\; X_+/\sqrt{N_+}]$ so that mid-sentence FSM states do not drown out prompt-end semantic boundaries.
    \item \textbf{Solve the High-Shrinkage Guard-Nullspace System} ($\lambda_s = 0.80$):
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    R &= (1-\lambda_s) X_p^\top X_p + \lambda_g \tilde{\alpha}_g X_{\mathrm{guard}}^\top X_{\mathrm{guard}} + \lambda_s \bar{c} I, \\
    k &= \operatorname{normalize}\big(R^{-1} X_p^\top y\big).
    \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
    \item \textbf{Calibrate the Gate Threshold via 2-Fold Out-of-Fold (OOF) Shrinkage}: Splitting $(X_+, X_{\mathrm{neg,sem}})$ into two halves to obtain out-of-fold directions $k^{(1)}, k^{(2)}$, we estimate the out-of-sample shrinkage factor $\rho = \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{oof}_+] / \mathbb{E}[\mathrm{in}_+]$ and set:
    \begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned} p_{\mathrm{low}} &= \rho \cdot Q_{0.08}(\mathrm{in}_+), \quad \theta = 0.65 n_{\max} + 0.35 p_{\mathrm{low}}, \\ s &= 16.0 / (p_{\mathrm{low}} - \theta). \end{aligned}
    \end{equation}
\end{enumerate}

\section{Experiments: In-Weight OS vs.\ SFT, Continual Learning, and Model Merging}

We evaluate three orthogonal post-training skills across both \texttt{Qwen3-8B} and \texttt{Meta-Llama-3.1-8B-Instruct}:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Skill 1: Surgical Safety \& Prompt-Injection Immunity (vs.\ \texttt{XSTest} Over-Refusal)}: Trained on $16$ adversarial jailbreak/override prompts and guarded against $16$ benign \texttt{XSTest}-style technical queries containing sensitive trigger words (\texttt{kill}, \texttt{terminate}, \texttt{exploit}, \texttt{crack}, \texttt{inject}, \texttt{dump}, \texttt{attack}, \texttt{root}). Evaluated on $20$ unseen jailbreaks and $20$ unseen \texttt{XSTest} benign technical prompts.
    \item \textbf{Skill 2: 3-Way Multi-Tool JSON Schema Compiler}: Routes user queries across three tools (\texttt{sql\_engine}, \texttt{python\_exec}, \texttt{web\_search}) while guarding against general mathematical/scientific questions. Evaluated on $24$ unseen tool queries and $50$ unseen hard math/code problems (\texttt{AMC}, \texttt{MATH-500}, \texttt{OlympiadBench}, \texttt{CRUXEval}).
    \item \textbf{Skill 3: System-1 / System-2 Metacognitive Compute Router}: Routes concise factual/arithmetic queries into a $12$-token direct answer (\texttt{25.7$\times$ token speedup}) while preserving full-depth System-2 CoT on the $50$ hard math/code problems.
\end{enumerate}

Table~\ref{tab:main_os} summarizes the results:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Eliminating SFT Over-Triggering}: Single-task Safety SFT causes $80\%\text{--}100\%$ false refusals on \texttt{XSTest} benign technical queries, while Single-Task Tool SFT causes $28\%\text{--}100\%$ false tool calls on general math. In-Weight OS achieves $\textbf{0.0\%}$ \texttt{XSTest} false refusals on both architectures.
    \item \textbf{Eliminating Catastrophic Forgetting \& Merging Collisions}: Sequential Continual SFT erases Skill 1 ($100\% \to 0\%$ on Llama-3.1; $100\% \to 25\%$ on Qwen3). \texttt{Task Arithmetic} and \texttt{TIES-Merging} suffer severe accuracy drops ($44.4\%\text{--}80.6\%$ mean success) due to sign and subspace collisions between dense task vectors. In contrast, In-Weight OS compiles all three skills into $51\text{--}57$ disjoint dormant neurons in $12\text{--}15$ seconds, achieving $\textbf{98.3\%}$ mean success on \texttt{Llama-3.1-8B} and $\textbf{100.0\%}$ on \texttt{Qwen3-8B}.
\end{itemize}

\section{Conclusion}
By partitioning dormant terminal-layer SwiGLU neurons into orthogonal, guard-nullspace-gated state machines, \textbf{In-Weight OS} enables zero-gradient, zero-interference multi-skill post-training that outperforms gradient fine-tuning, continual learning, and state-of-the-art model merging across architectures.
